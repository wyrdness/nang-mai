## Overview

Nang Mai (Thai: นางไม้, 'Lady of the Tree') is the generic Thai folkloric category for female ghosts or spirits that inhabit large trees. Specific named tree spirits — most prominently Nang Tani (of the wild Musa acuminata banana tree) and Nang Ta-khian (of the takhian, Hopea odorata) — are understood as particular instances of Nang Mai. Nang Mai are typically described as young women in pleated traditional Thai dress with shoulder-length hair, said to inhabit invisible 'palaces' within the canopies of their trees. The male equivalent is the Rukkhadeva (รุกขเทวดา), a Pali-derived term for a tree deity who tends to inhabit larger trees such as the banyan. Beliefs around Nang Mai are pragmatic as well as devotional: they are invoked to deter the felling of large trees, with folklore warning that a tree-cutter who disturbs a Nang Mai's home will be cursed, sicken, or lose their mind. Modern offerings include traditional Thai costumes, perfumes, and cosmetic kits, and Thai villagers commonly mark spirit trees with multicoloured (often three-coloured) cloth bands. The tradition is integrated with Theravada Buddhist practice in Thailand: monastic precepts forbidding the cutting of fresh vegetation are widely understood to honor tree spirits, and folk practice happily compromises between formal Buddhism and local animist veneration.
